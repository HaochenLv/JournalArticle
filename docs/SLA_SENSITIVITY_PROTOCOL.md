# Stage 4: frozen SLA-regime sensitivity protocol

This protocol is designed after Stage 3 (source commit `69a6b175b5be6788b6448f23edbf1366c1f626c3`). It is a controlled parameter sensitivity supplement on the same six Stage 3 held-out workloads, **not an independent held-out workload evaluation**. Forty-eight workload/SLA conditions are not 48 independent workloads. The machine-readable authority is `config/sla_sensitivity_protocol.json`; its canonical and byte hashes, environment and source manifest are recorded before new outcomes in `results/sla_sensitivity/protocol_snapshot.json`.

Only primary heterogeneous h101–h106, their original links and shifts [-2,-1,0,1,2] are allowed. Original common-grid sizes are 51,53,46,41,49,50 (1,450 physical points). All model, workload, partition, profile, memory, network, topology, accounting and event-processing inputs remain frozen. Raw uses fixed_in_progress=False; Legacy=True; both use fixed overhead .005 seconds and zero queue overhead.

The eight (TTFT,TPOT) pairs in seconds are (5.2,.3), (5.2,.6), (5.2,1.2), (5.2,2.4), (5.2,5), (5.2,10), (4.68,1.2), (4.68,10). Use numeric labels without presupposing violation mechanism. Every SLA/semantic pair actually calls JB1; early-stopped evaluator maxima are never reused to classify a new SLA. Existing reference metrics are reclassified without another physical simulation or another overhead charge. Preserve the evaluator's first complete violation set; do not infer causal ordering from the compact reference adapter.

Before refinement, both old SLA labels and decisions for Raw, Legacy and reference must exactly reproduce Stage 3 on its original grid. SLA relaxation may not turn safe to unsafe. Any inconsistency is debugged before refinement.

Refinement collects the union of every adjacent safe/unsafe change in both directions across all candidates, SLAs and three models. If upper/lower-1 > .025, propose round(sqrt(lower*upper),10), retaining the Stage 3 numeric precision. Rank by descending log width, then ascending lower endpoint. A selected intensity is shared by every candidate and SLA; run HELIX once per physical point and classify all eight thresholds. Never expand the original range or choose only mismatches/winners. Missing/failed labels stay null and do not fabricate transitions; report them as unresolved evidence.

Hard limits: 24 added intensities per workload, 120 unique new physical points per workload, 720 overall; at most six rounds. Stop on no unresolved transition, budget exhaustion, six rounds, or no legal midpoint. Record all unselected/final unresolved transitions. A failed reference attempt has a 600-second killable deadline and one identical-input retry. Failure never means unsafe; successful simulations must fully drain. Reuse Stage 3 memory-aware scheduling unchanged: ceiling 12 workers, 24 GiB estimated budget, 6 GiB live reserve, ceil((512+.30*output_tokens)/256)*256 MiB reservation per job. Portable execution must verify frozen dependency sources and original evaluator outputs before new physical runs; host/environment changes are disclosed.

Tie-break: smallest absolute shift, then negative. Preserve all best-set ties. Agreement means selected belongs to reference-best; disjoint best sets mean strict mismatch; overlapping best sets with selected outside reference-best mean tie-break mismatch. Abstention/no-safe/failed cases remain separate. Sampled capacity is the largest observed safe intensity, not a continuous bound or safe prefix. Keep all sequences, transitions, no-safe, right-censoring, nonmonotonicity and unresolved brackets. Undefined quantities remain null.

For B=reference-best sampled capacity, S=selected-partition reference capacity and L=recommended load, report partition loss 1-S/B, operating loss 1-L/B and allocation (S-L)/B. Only partition+allocation equals operating loss. B=0 makes relative quality undefined. Exact unsafe recommendation has safety-adjusted usable load 0; this says nothing about other loads. Report exact safety, request violations, separate TTFT/TPOT exceedance and violation type.

Outputs are isolated under `results/sla_sensitivity/`: immutable protocol/source snapshots, point records, raw.csv, judgment.csv, capacity.csv (including complete sequences), decisions.csv, operating_points.csv, execution_summary.json, summary.json, quality_checks.json, refinement_history.json and failed_points.json. Reports: `docs/SLA_REGIME_SENSITIVITY.md` and `SLA_SENSITIVITY_EVIDENCE_REPORT.md`; at most one new main-text candidate figure. Stage 2/3 protected files are never rewritten. Logs and outline may receive explicit Stage 4 additions.

No outcome trend is presumed. Counts on adaptive grids are descriptive, not production risk probabilities. Do not expand workload/model/GPU/network/profile/algorithm/candidate/SLA scope. JB1 is the journal's explicitly specified lightweight evaluator; Legacy is an implementation-semantic control. The withdrawn unpublished AICCC manuscript is not prior publication. The accepted *Evaluator-Guided SLA-Aware Layer Partitioning for Heterogeneous LLM Pipelines* is prior work: the shift family, evaluator-as-score and search are not this stage's contribution. This stage contributes only SLA-regime sensitivity evidence, not manuscript prose.
